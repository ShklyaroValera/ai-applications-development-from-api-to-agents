# Style Guide Reference

## Brand Voice at a Glance

| Attribute      | Do                                                  | Don't                                           |
|----------------|-----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Voice          | Friendly, confident, human                          | Stiff, bureaucratic, overly salesy              |
| Tone           | Adapt to the moment: upbeat, calm, or neutral       | Sarcasm, guilt-tripping, fake urgency           |
| Sentences      | Short (under 20 words), one idea each               | Long chains of clauses and nested qualifiers    |
| Vocabulary     | Plain, everyday words ("use", "help", "start")      | Jargon ("utilize", "facilitate", "leverage")    |
| Point of view  | "You" for the reader, "we" for the company          | "The user", "the customer", "Company X"         |
| Contractions   | Use them: "you're", "we'll", "don't"                | "You are", "we will", "do not" in casual copy   |

## Content Types

### Emails

**Subject lines**
- 6-8 words, under 50 characters.
- Sentence case; state the benefit or the key fact.
- Avoid: ALL CAPS, "!!!", "RE:"/"FWD:" tricks, spam words ("free", "act now", "urgent"),
  and vague openers ("Update", "Newsletter #12").

**Body**
- Open with the most important point in the first sentence.
- 3-5 short paragraphs maximum; use bullets for three or more items.
- One primary call to action, written as a verb-first button or link: "Reset your password",
  "Book a demo". Never "Click here".
- Sign-off: "Thanks," or "Cheers," followed by a real name or "The [Team] team".
  No "Best regards, Management".

**Tone dial**
| Type          | Tone                         | Example opener                               |
|---------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Transactional | Clear, neutral, brief        | "Your order #4821 is on its way."            |
| Marketing     | Warm, upbeat, benefit-led    | "Your weekends just got easier."             |
| Support       | Calm, empathetic, helpful    | "Sorry about the trouble. Here's the fix."   |

### Blog Posts

**Structure**
- One H1 (the title), H2 for main sections, H3 only when needed. Never skip levels.
- Intro: 2-3 sentences that state the problem and what the reader will get.
- End with a short summary or a clear next step.

**Style**
- Aim for a grade 8 reading level.
- Spell out acronyms on first use: "search engine optimization (SEO)".
- Use examples, lists, and short paragraphs to break up text.

**Headings**
- Sentence case.
- Outcome-focused: tell the reader what they'll get ("Cut your build time in half"),
  not a label ("Build optimization").

### Social Media

**LinkedIn**
- Up to 3,000 characters, but aim for 150-300 words.
- The first line is the hook (it's all people see before "see more"): a question, a number,
  or a bold statement. No "We are excited to announce".
- 3-5 relevant hashtags at the end, never in the middle of sentences.
- Emojis: 0-2, only if they add meaning.

**Twitter / X**
- 280 characters max; aim for 200 or less to leave room for replies and quotes.
- Lead with the point; one idea per post; use a thread for more.
- 1-2 hashtags max.
- Emojis: 0-1.

### Support / Help Content

**Tone**
- Empathy first: acknowledge the problem in one sentence ("Sorry you ran into this").
- Solution first: give the fix right after, before any background or explanation.
- Never blame the customer ("you failed to", "you should have").

**Language**
- Use partnering phrases: "Let's fix this together", "Here's what we can do", "I'll check that for you".
- Avoid "unfortunately", "as per our policy", and "please be advised".

**Format**
- Numbered steps for any procedure, one action per step.
- **Bold** UI elements exactly as they appear: click **Settings** > **Billing**.
- Close with an offer of further help: "If that doesn't do it, just reply and we'll take another look."

## Grammar & Mechanics

**Punctuation**
- Oxford comma: always ("email, chat, and phone").
- Em dash without spaces for asides—like this—but no more than one pair per paragraph.
- Exclamation marks: at most one per piece of content, never in support replies about problems.

**Capitalization**
- Sentence case for headings, subject lines, buttons, and menu items.
- Product names exactly as branded; don't capitalize generic features ("dashboard", "reports").

**Numbers**
- Spell out one to nine; use digits for 10 and above.
- Always digits for prices ($5), percentages (5%), dates (March 3), times (9 am), and versions.
- Use commas in numbers of 1,000 and above.

**Inclusive language**
- Use "they/them" when gender is unknown; address groups as "everyone" or "folks", not "guys".
- Prefer neutral terms: "allowlist/denylist", "primary/replica", "staff the booth".
- Describe people, not conditions; avoid ableist idioms ("crazy", "lame", "blind spot").
