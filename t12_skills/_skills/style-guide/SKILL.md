## Workflow

1. **Identify the content type**: email, blog post, social post (which platform), or support reply.
   If the user only asks a question about the rules, answer it from REFERENCE.md and stop.
2. **Apply the core principles** below to the draft.
3. **Apply the type-specific rules** from the matching section of REFERENCE.md
   (length limits, structure, CTA, hashtags, etc.).
4. **Check EXAMPLES.md** for the closest before/after pattern and match that level of change.
5. **Run the Instant Rules checklist** below.
6. **Return** the polished text first, then a short **Changes** list (3-5 bullets) explaining
   what you changed and which rule you applied.

## Core Principles

- **Voice**: friendly, confident, and human. We sound like a knowledgeable colleague, not a
  corporation or a legal department.
- **Tone**: adapts to context. Warm and upbeat for marketing, calm and empathetic for support,
  clear and neutral for transactional messages. Never sarcastic, never pushy.
- **Length**: shorter is better. One idea per sentence, sentences under 20 words, paragraphs of
  1-3 sentences. Cut every word that doesn't add meaning.
- **Person**: speak to the reader as "you"; refer to the company as "we". Avoid "the user",
  "the customer", or the company name in the third person.

## Instant Rules

- [ ] No jargon or corporate filler ("leverage", "synergy", "we are pleased to inform you").
      See the jargon table in EXAMPLES.md.
- [ ] Active voice: "We fixed the bug", not "The bug has been fixed by our team".
- [ ] Use contractions: "you're", "we'll", "it's", "don't".
- [ ] Numbers: spell out one to nine, digits for 10 and above; always digits for prices,
      percentages, dates, and times.
- [ ] Oxford comma in lists of three or more.
- [ ] At most one exclamation mark per piece; no "!!!" and no ALL CAPS for emphasis.
- [ ] Sentence case for headings and subject lines.
- [ ] Lead with the most important information or the answer.
